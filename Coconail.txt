As part of the current implementation assessment, we are reviewing the existing Adobe Launch and Adobe Analytics setup in detail to build a comprehensive audit and tagging specification that can be used as the baseline for the Web SDK migration.
The review involves more than validating the tags that are currently firing. For each applicable page and user interaction, we are tracing the complete implementation flow, including the Launch rule being triggered, associated data elements, data-layer or page-level source of each value, Analytics request and event configuration, and any implementation dependencies. We are also capturing supporting evidence and documenting issues or unexpected behaviour identified during the analysis.
Since the objective is to cover the solution end to end, the effort will also include reviewing the relevant URLs, page-load events, clicks and other tracked interactions, as well as identifying shared rules, reusable components, iframe or embedded application dependencies, and any inconsistencies in the current implementation. The recent Offers-page analysis is one example where a detailed review helped us identify an additional iframe and separate Launch-property dependency that was not immediately visible from the initial tagging review.
Based on the current understanding of the scope, our initial estimate for completing the first comprehensive version of the audit is approximately 3–4 weeks, with the work broadly progressing as follows:
Week 1 – Discovery and inventory: Identify the key URLs, user journeys, events, Launch rules, data elements and existing Analytics dependencies that need to be covered.
Week 2–3 – Detailed implementation analysis: Validate page views and interactions, trace data sources and rule execution, review Analytics requests, capture evidence, and document implementation gaps or observations.
Week 4 – Consolidation and validation: Complete the tagging specification/audit workbook, perform cross-checks for coverage and consistency, document identified issues and dependencies, and prepare the consolidated findings.
This timeline should be considered an initial estimate rather than a fixed commitment, as additional complexity may be identified while tracing the current implementation. For example, embedded applications, multiple Launch properties, custom-code rules, shared data elements, or undocumented tracking dependencies can require additional investigation.
Our priority is to ensure that the audit is sufficiently detailed and accurate to serve as a reliable reference for the Web SDK migration, rather than limiting the analysis purely to meet a timeline. We will continue to share key findings during the assessment rather than waiting until the final document is complete.
